Ways to indicate a connection between two nodes in the same column

The columns are vertical stacks of horizontal bars, and the existing cross-column edges already live on the inner sides (Themes on their right toward Projects, Skills on their left). So a same-column link reads most clearly when it uses a different visual channel — the outer edge, a neutral (non-source) color, or no line at all. Each mock links the 2nd and 4th bar as an example.

recommended

1 · Outer-edge bracket

A thin brace on the column's outer edge (the side with no cross-column ribbons) ties the two bars together, like a tree/phylogeny brace.

  • + Never collides with the source-colored edges.
  • + Reads instantly as "these two relate."
  • − Needs a little outer margin reserved.
  • − Crossing brackets get busy if many pairs.

Best for: a handful of explicit pairings you always want visible.

2 · Loop-out ribbon

A curved ribbon leaves one bar, bows out into the margin, and returns to the other — the same visual language as your cross-column ribbons, kept on one side.

  • + Visually consistent with existing edges.
  • + Can carry a color/gradient for meaning.
  • − Eats more horizontal margin than a bracket.
  • − Two nearby loops can overlap awkwardly.

Best for: making same-column links feel native to the map.

3 · Inset spine & stubs

A short vertical line runs inside the bars near their edge, with little stubs into each linked node. No extra margin needed.

  • + Zero horizontal footprint.
  • + Subtle; won't dominate the map.
  • − Competes with the node's own outline/text.
  • − Weaker signal — easy to miss.

Best for: dense layouts where margin is scarce.

4 · Group frame

Wrap a set of adjacent bars in a labelled rounded frame — grouping rather than pairwise linking.

  • + Great for "these belong together" clusters.
  • + Can carry a group label.
  • − Only works if members sit next to each other.
  • − Can't show a link that skips a node.

Best for: sub-grouping consecutive nodes (needs stable ordering).

5 · Shared tag (no line)

Give each partner the same little chip (a color, letter, or number). No drawn line at all — the eye pairs them by matching mark.

  • + Works for non-adjacent nodes, any distance.
  • + Scales to many independent pairs/sets.
  • − Requires scanning to find the match.
  • − Runs out of distinct chips if there are many.

Best for: several far-apart relationships without clutter.

6 · Hover-only highlight

Nothing persistent: hovering (or selecting) a node brightens it and its same-column partners while the rest dim — reusing your existing hover-highlight machinery.

  • + Zero static clutter; scales to any number of links.
  • + Cheap — extends the highlight you already have.
  • − Invisible until the user interacts.
  • − No use on a static export / screenshot.

Best for: many links you only need on demand (pairs well with 1 or 5).

My take: combine #1 (outer bracket) or #5 (shared tag) for the persistent signal with #6 (hover highlight) for detail on demand. Brackets for a few always-on pairings; tags when partners are far apart or numerous; hover to confirm without cluttering the static view.
